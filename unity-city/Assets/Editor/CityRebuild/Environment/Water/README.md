# Harbour water authoring

Integration: disable only the old `CityMesh_130` renderer and call
`BraxCityWaterBuild.Apply(cityRoot)` after the city's static lighting bake and
before saving the generated scene. Keep the original glTF, material and texture.

The original extent is Unity X 84..884, Z -340..460, mean Y -1.7 m. The generator
retains that footprint. Eight small patches near the quay use a four-metre grid;
three flat patches cover the remainder. Total generated geometry is 11 renderers.
The shared material has one opaque pass and no grab pass or camera depth texture.
No camera, light, probe, collider or runtime component is created.

### Realistic water (MIASTO R1, 18.11.2026)

The owner asked: "nie możemy zrobić bardziej realistycznej wody?" and "woda… nie pasek… widać nic".
The same material also drives the sea behind the camera (`BraxMorzeZaKamera`) and
the island's open sea (a copy of this material), so every sea surface of the city
changes together.

- **Waves.** The broad analytic swell is unchanged (the same phases as `BraxVessel`).
  It now carries two tiles of a wind-wave normal map (10 m and 23 m, rotated 36.9°,
  each drifting its own way). The normal map is an FFT height field with a
  Phillips spectrum (Tessendorf 2001). `unity-tools/claude/woda_fale.py fale`
  reproduces `Assets/Source/Claude/Miasto/woda/fale_normal.png` from a fixed seed.
- **Reflection.** The water reflects the city sky panorama itself: the same
  texture, rotation and exposure as the skybox. `BraxRegionHoryzont` calls
  `BraxCityWaterBuild.UstawNiebo`. The Fresnel term uses Schlick with n = 1.33
  (F0 = 0.02). The sun highlight is energy-normalised Blinn-Phong.
- **Below the surface.**
  - `BraxWodaDno` bakes a depth texture (R8, sqrt(depth / 20 m), 1024 × 768) over
    x 60..1468, z -452..572. It uses the beach terrain function
    (`BraxNabrzezePlaza.Wysokosc`), the island terrain (`BraxMiastoWyspa.H`) and a
    harbour model. At the quay walls that model drops straight to the collision
    seabed at y -7.
  - The view ray is refracted (Snell, 1/1.33) to the seabed. Sand
    (`damp_sand`, tinted like the wet beach sand) is lit by the refracted sun and
    by caustics, then attenuated per metre in RGB (0.45, 0.09, 0.075).
    `woda_fale.py kaustyki` computes the caustics by refracting sun rays through
    the same kind of wave spectrum. Light scattered by deep water fills in as the
    transmittance falls.
  - In shallow water the sand shows through: turquoise near the beach, blue
    further out, dark at the 5 m quays.
- **Cost.** Two normal-map samples, one depth sample and one sky sample per pixel.
  Water shallower than 9 m adds one more depth sample, one sand sample and two
  caustics samples; this extra work runs in a coherent dynamic branch with
  explicit gradients.

## Assets and review status

The wave normal map and the caustics are generated by our own script from a
physical wave spectrum (no third-party texture). The seabed sand is the same
CC0 Poly Haven `damp_sand` photoscan that the beach already uses.

The generator validates imported shader errors, triangle winding, mean level,
coverage and absence of extra render cameras/lights/probes/colliders. This does
not replace WebGL shader compilation, visual review while moving, or a physical
phone performance test. Review the quay, pier edge and water/sky horizon in a real
Unity build. Check long-session motion precision, foreground occlusion, seams at
patch boundaries and day-time reflection intensity before accepting its look.
